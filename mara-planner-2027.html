Food guide

Kenyan cooking is hearty and communal, with a deep Indian influence that will feel familiar to your parents — chapati, samosa, pilau and biryani all appear. Your no-beef rule is easy here: goat, chicken and fish carry most of the good stuff.

🔥 The one thing to eat

Nyama choma — "roasted meat," and less a dish than a national weekend ritual. Meat goes on charcoal seasoned with nothing but salt, cooks low and slow, and arrives on a wooden board to be pulled apart by hand.

Order it as goat, which is the traditional and most common choice anyway, or chicken. Beef is only one option among several, so skipping it costs you nothing.

It comes with ugali and kachumbari, and a cold Tusker is the standard partner.

🍽️ Eating on safari

Your camps are full board, and safari kitchens are used to accommodating dietary requirements — vegetarian, halal, no-beef, all routine. Flag it at booking and again on arrival.

Expect cooked breakfasts, packed bush breakfasts on dawn drives, buffet lunches and three-course dinners. Day 5's balloon includes the traditional champagne bush breakfast on the plains.

Camp water is filtered and bottled water is everywhere; stick to it over tap.

The staples

What you'll see on almost every table.

Ugali maize staple
Firm maize-flour dough you tear and use to scoop stew and greens. Neutral, filling, the backbone of the plate. no beef
Kachumbari fresh side
Bright salsa of tomato, red onion, coriander, chilli and lime. Cuts through everything grilled. veg
Sukuma wiki greens
Collard greens with onion and tomato. The name means "stretch the week" — the everyday vegetable. veg
Githeri maize & beans
Boiled maize and kidney beans, Kikuyu in origin, now eaten countrywide. Simple and hearty. veg
Irio / Mukimo mash
Potato mashed with peas, maize and greens. Comforting, and a good side with grilled chicken. veg
Chapati flatbread
Thicker and flakier than the Indian version. Excellent torn into stew. veg
Pilau & biryani spiced rice
Aromatic with cumin, cardamom and cinnamon; strongest on the coast. Ask for the chicken or goat version. ask chicken
Matoke banana stew
Savoury green-banana stew, thick and mild. veg
Tilapia freshwater fish
Whole fried or stewed lake fish, usually with ugali. A Nairobi favourite and a great no-beef main. no beef
Mandazi snack
Lightly sweet fried dough, the East African doughnut. Perfect with chai. veg
Kenyan chai drink
Tea boiled directly in milk and water with sugar. Strong and sweet — very close to home for your parents.

💡 Practical notes

Food is mild to moderate by default. Ask for pilipili if you want heat.

Eating with your hands is normal with ugali and nyama choma — wash up before and after.

Vegetarians are very well served: maharagwe (beans), ndengu, githeri, irio, vegetable pilau, bhajia and viazi karai are all common.

Halal food is widely available in Nairobi and on the coast.

What to actually buy

The things worth suitcase space.

Kazuri beads
Hand-painted ceramic jewellery. The signature Nairobi buy, ethically made, and light to pack.
Kanga & kitenge
Vivid printed cloth sold by the metre. Endlessly useful, weighs nothing, and each kanga carries a printed Swahili proverb.
Kiondo baskets
Handwoven sisal baskets with leather trim. Beautiful, practical, and they pack flat-ish.
Kisii soapstone
Smooth carved bowls and sculptures in pale stone. Distinctly Kenyan — but heavy, so choose carefully.
Maasai beadwork
Necklaces, belts and beaded sandals. Best value at Kariokor, best quality at the cooperatives.
Kenyan coffee & AA tea
Excellent, cheap, and the easiest gifts to bring back for everyone else.
Tsavorite
A green garnet found in Kenya and nowhere much else. Buy only from reputable, certified dealers — ask for paperwork.
Bargaining: at markets like Kariokor, haggling is expected and good-natured — start well below the asking price and settle near the middle. At cooperatives and craft centres (Kazuri, Utamaduni, Amani ya Juu) prices are fixed and fair, so don't negotiate; the whole point is that the makers are paid properly.

Entry & essentials

The paperwork is light. Here's the checklist, plus the booking clock.

🛂 Kenya eTA — all three of you

No sticker visa since 2024 — everyone needs the online Electronic Travel Authorisation, Indian and British passports alike.

Cost~$30–34 pp Apply atetakenya.go.ke (official only) When≥3 days before — do it ~2 weeks out NeedPassport 6mo+ valid, photo, itinerary, camp booking ValiditySingle entry, up to 90 days
Enter passport and payment details yourselves on the government site — I can talk you through the form, but those fields are yours to fill.

💉 Health

Malaria: the Mara is a risk area — see a travel clinic 4–6 weeks before about prophylaxis.

Yellow fever: generally not required arriving direct from India or the UK. Confirm nearer the time.

Insurance: essential, and check it covers safari activities, balloon flights, and medical evacuation — some policies exclude ballooning.

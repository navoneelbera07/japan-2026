From the Field

Real tips from someone who just got back. No theory — things they wish they'd known and things they were glad they knew.

The Single Best Piece of Advice

"Pretty much any shop, stall or restaurant run by one or two old people (probably less than 4ft tall) will possibly be the best experience of your life. The excitement they show when you genuinely appreciate their product will be a magical experience — antique shops, a little old lady who dyed the fabric herself, a family-owned ramen shop. They put decades of expertise into what they make and it shows. The trendy place with a long queue might be good, but the single old man slinging soba in the tiniest restaurant will be the best."

Use AirPod live translation for these interactions. Bow a lot. Sumimasen and arigato gozaimasu your way through a great time.

🌅
Get Up Early
Tokyo is quiet and peaceful in the morning. Get up, walk to where you want to be, hit 7-Eleven for an egg sandwich and your morning potions, start your 30,000-step day. Coffee shops don't open until 11am — you'll survive.
🚇
Shinkansen Exit Stress
"Exiting the train station after the Shinkansen — Jesus Christ, more stressful than getting on the right train." QR code? Insert ticket? Both? Each station is different. Have your QR code ready, insert both tickets if you have two, ask staff if confused. YouTube it beforehand.
🚶
Walking Side
People walk on the left. Except in Kyoto where they walk on the right. Just follow locals and keep your eyes up — everyone's on their phones.
🧴
Pharmacy & 7-Eleven
The pharmacy has literally everything for anything. Drink the 7-Eleven potions: Fiber, Vitamin C, D, smoothies. Get the heated eye mask and foot cooling pads. The pharmacy is cheaper than the konbini for most health items.
👜
Reusable Bag
Bring a reusable bag everywhere. When offered a plastic bag say "daijobu desu" (no thank you). It earns smiles. Say "dozo" when letting someone ahead of you in a queue.
🧼
Hand Soap & Towel
Bathrooms are excellent but carry a small hand soap and a hand towel everywhere. Many public bathrooms have no soap or hand dryers.
🏟️
Baseball Game
"On par with a soccer game in London. Best experience of my life." Find ticket release dates online — actually easy to book as a non-local but you have to be quick. Don't miss this if you can squeeze it in.
💆
Head Spa
"Amazing. Loved it. I'd do it again." Japanese head spas are a genuine experience — scalp treatment, wash, massage. About ¥5,000–8,000 for an hour. Book in advance at a local salon.
🌙
Night Illuminations
Check which shrines have night illuminations while you're there. In Kyoto: Yasaka Shrine and Fushimi Inari at night — hardly any people, and at Fushimi Inari you might see wild boar on the back trail.
🏯
Skip the Obvious Tourist Traps
"Samurai ninja museum is a no go my bro. Just go to a real museum." The sumo experience was fun but basically a comedy show. Spend the time in parks instead.
🏞️
Shinjuku Gyoen + Meiji
"Best days were spent in the parks. We spent a full day going from Shinjuku Gyoen to Meiji Jingu and grabbing lunch in between. Highly recommend the early start." Already in your plan — this is the move.
🍵
Kyoto Hidden Coffee
Near Hokan-ji (Yasaka Pagoda), KeFu coffee stand opens at 7am. Excellent coffee for early morning temple-goers. The kind of place that doesn't exist on any tourist list.
"If a street feels too touristy, turn down the next alley and go a street over. Look for small chalkboards with no English and a price. It will be something excellent." This is the best navigation strategy for both Tokyo and Kyoto.
Shinkansen seat reality check: One traveller regretted buying shinkansen tickets in advance on Klook — the QR code process at ticket gates was frustrating and they ended up buying at the station instead. Non-reserved cars were "honestly just as good." However, for the Gran Class leg (28 May), reserving in advance is essential — only 18 seats. For other legs, buying at the station a day before is completely fine.

Trains & Passes

The One Pass You Need

Hokuriku Arch Pass — ¥35,000 — Buy Before You Travel

  • Foreign tourists only (temporary visitor visa). You qualify.
  • Valid for 7 consecutive days from activation date. Activate on 28 May (runs 28 May–3 Jun).
  • Covers: Narita Express N'EX · Hokuriku Shinkansen (Tokyo→Kanazawa) · Thunderbird limited express (Kanazawa→Kyoto) · JR lines within Tokyo and Kansai
  • Does NOT cover: Tokyo subway (use Suica) · Gran Class upgrade (pay separately) · Green Car upgrade (pay separately) · Tokaido Shinkansen · Kyoto→Kinosaki (buy individually)
  • Where to buy: jreast.co.jp/en or Klook. You receive a Paper Exchange Order. Bring it to Narita JR office on 23 May to collect the physical pass.
Maths check: Individual fares for your pass-covered legs total ~¥34,100 (Narita N'EX ¥3,250 + Tokyo→Kanazawa ¥14,380 + Thunderbird ¥7,720 + Tokyo JR days ¥5,000 + return N'EX ¥3,250). Pass costs ¥35,000 — essentially breaks even on fares alone, and includes total flexibility plus free seat reservations on all legs.

What to Do on 23 May at Narita Airport — Most Important Train Actions

Do all of this at the JR East Travel Service Center in the Narita arrivals hall before leaving the airport. It will take 20–30 minutes and saves headaches for the whole trip.
  1. Hand over your Paper Exchange Order + passport → receive physical Hokuriku Arch Pass
  2. Tell them: activate date 28 May
  3. Book free seat reservations: N'EX to Tokyo (today), Kagayaki 28 May, Thunderbird 31 May
  4. Pay Gran Class surcharge and book Gran Class seat on Kagayaki for 28 May — only 18 seats, do this immediately

Luggage Forwarding (Takuhaibin)

What is Takuhaibin?

Japan's nationwide door-to-door luggage delivery system. You send your suitcase to your next hotel the day before you travel and arrive hands-free. ~¥2,500–3,000 per bag per leg. Virtually never lost or damaged. Look for the Yamato Transport yellow van with the black cat logo — you'll see them everywhere.

The magic phrase: Walk to your hotel front desk, say "Takuhaibin, onegaishimasu" (tah-kyoo-bin, oh-neh-gah-ee-shi-mah-su). They handle everything. You just pay.
